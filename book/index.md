## About this book

Every field that applies machine learning builds up a body of reference
literature: the papers that introduced its benchmark datasets, its standard
architectures and its software. These papers are cited in nearly every new
result, yet the knowledge of which paper goes with which dataset, which
version of a record was used, and what a benchmark can and cannot show is
mostly passed on by word of mouth. This book writes that knowledge down.

Each chapter covers one topic. It describes the items the field relies on,
states how each was produced and what it is used for, and gives the paper
that introduced it together with the record that holds its files or code.
The parts group the chapters by field, starting with machine learning for
particle physics.

## How to read it

Sections are numbered within chapters (1.2, 1.2.1), as are tables, so that
any statement can be referred to precisely. Boxed environments set apart
material of a particular kind:

Definition
:   A term fixed for use throughout the book.

Remark
:   A caveat or pitfall worth remembering when using an item.

Warning
:   A property of an item that silently changes what a result on it means.

Citations appear as bracketed numbers and resolve to the reference list at
the end of each chapter, which gives the entry to cite. Bibliographic
details follow the records of [INSPIRE-HEP](https://inspirehep.net/) for
particle-physics papers and the publisher's record otherwise; descriptions
of datasets follow the documentation in their own records. Each chapter
states the month in which its entries were last checked against those
sources.
