---
title: Machine Learning for Particle Physics
summary: "The reference literature of machine learning in particle physics: the public datasets models are trained and compared on, and the papers that introduced them."
---

Machine learning entered particle physics through classification tasks such
as jet tagging and event selection, and now reaches every stage of the
analysis chain: reconstruction, fast simulation, anomaly detection,
unfolding and statistical inference. Results in each of these areas are
compared on a small number of shared datasets, released by phenomenology
groups, by the experimental collaborations, and through community
challenges.

The chapters in this part collect that literature by topic. They begin with
the public datasets, because a dataset fixes what a result can claim: the
physics processes, the level of detector simulation, and the task against
which a model is judged.
